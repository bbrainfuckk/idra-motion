# How the LetAIApply trailer was made in one shot

[Watch it on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0TXt47MJRPY). 54.5 seconds, 1920 x 1080, a 60 fps
master and a 30 fps web cut (9.7 MB), nine narration lines, six sound effects, music written in code.
One HTML file of about 740 lines, rendered on a laptop in about seven minutes.

No video editor, no keyframe app, no stock footage, no stock music. This page is the method, step by
step, including what feedback changed between the first and the final cut.

## The one idea

**Every frame is a function of time.** The page has one function, `seek(t)`, that sets every element on
screen from the number `t`. There are no CSS transitions, no CSS animations, no timers and no state
carried from one frame to the next. That single rule is what makes everything else work:

- The renderer can ask for any frame, in any order, and get the same pixels every time.
- Four browser windows can film four parts of the video at once and the parts join seamlessly.
- Motion blur is free: film each frame a few times a fraction apart and average them.
- The sound can be built from the same timing tables as the picture, so a click lands on its frame.
- You can scrub to any moment in a browser and see exactly what the render will show.

## The steps

### 1. A beat sheet before any code

Every line of narration gets a row: when it starts, what is said, what the viewer sees, what you hear.
The rule for the picture column: **whatever is said must visibly happen.** If the voice says "LinkedIn,
Indeed or JobStreet", three tabs open. If it says "it stops at Submit", the camera pushes in on the Submit
button. The rule for claims: only what the product really does, said the way its own site says it.

An excerpt of the real one (it lives as a comment at the top of the page):

```

 3.76 / 5.14 / 6.36  "Again." x3        camera pulls back: 9, 40, 150 forms  whoosh + impact
 7.36  the wall of forms folds to a dot; the dot floods the frame white      riser, impact
 8.05  "LetAIApply fills them for you." logo, name, line                     pad enters
17.30  "Open a job on LinkedIn, Indeed, or JobStreet, and press Apply."
                                        an address is typed, each site's tab opens, the card lands on each
29.75  "And it stops at Submit."        push in on the button, marching ring  pop
```

### 2. Narration first, then the picture

The voice sets the rhythm, so it is recorded first and the picture is timed to it, never the other way.

- One audio file per line, generated by ElevenLabs in three voices. They were played one at a time and
  the client picked one (Matilda). The trailer paid for them with free TTSWP credits
  (`tools/ttswp-say.php`); `tools/vo.py make` does the same with your own ElevenLabs key. Both are in
  [narration.md](narration.md).
- Every line levelled to the same loudness (`tools/vo.py level`).
- Every word's start and end time, found by Whisper listening to the lines (`tools/vo.py words`).
  Whisper only times the words; the voice is ElevenLabs. The picture's beats use these times: the
  word "Again." appears on screen at the moment it is said, three times, a little bigger each time.
- A table of line start times drives both the sound and the picture. When a line was added in the
  middle (the Autopilot line), every later beat moved by one constant instead of being retimed by hand.

### 3. The product, rebuilt in the page

The interface on screen is real HTML and CSS at full size, in the product's own font, with its own logo,
filled with a made-up person and company. A camera layer (`cam(layer, x, y, zoom)`) pushes in on the
part that matters. Because it is rebuilt rather than screen-recorded, every field can type itself at an
exact time and the camera can land on a pixel.

### 4. A small motion vocabulary, used everywhere

- **Springs** for anything that arrives: cards, words, logos (`spring`, `track`).
- **Easing** for anything designed: camera moves, wipes, a highlight box drawing itself (`ease`, `kf`).
- **Typed text** with a caret that only shows while typing (`typed`), timed from a table.
- **Counters** that roll (`count`), **staggers** (each item a few hundredths of a second later), and a
  **cursor** that moves on an eased path and dips in scale on the click.
- **Seeded randomness** (`rng`): a wall of 150 forms scattered "at random" is the same on every frame.

### 5. Sound from the same tables

The page builds its own soundtrack (`window.buildAudio`, `engine/sound.js`) on an OfflineAudioContext:

- Key ticks come from the same (start, length, speed) rows that type the text, so every key lands.
- Whooshes, pops, clicks and impacts are placed at the same times the picture uses.
- Three buses: narration at the front, effects under it, music about 12 dB under the narration and
  dipping while someone talks.
- The music is synthesised: a pad through a simple progression, a soft pluck, a low drone for the dark
  opening. Nothing to license, nothing a platform can match against.

### 6. Check stills before rendering

`render.mjs ... --stills 0.9,8.2,20.6,...` films one frame at every beat into a contact sheet in seconds.
Read every word on screen against the narration, check every claim, check that what is said is visible.
Only then render the whole thing.

### 7. Render

`render.mjs` opens headless Chrome or Edge, gives each worker its own window (a background tab stops
painting), films JPEG frames and pipes them into ffmpeg, joins the parts, renders the page's soundtrack,
measures it and moves it to -16 LUFS with one fixed gain and a limiter at -2 dBFS. A 30 fps draft of the
trailer takes under a minute; the 60 fps master with 4 sub-frames of motion blur about seven.

### 8. Ship

A 60 fps master, a 30 fps web cut small enough for upload limits (under 10 MB), a poster frame, and a
credits file listing every sound's licence and the voice provider.

## What feedback changed (first cut to final)

| Feedback | Change |
| --- | --- |
| "When it said LinkedIn, Indeed or JobStreet, nothing changed." | An address is typed and each site's tab really opens, each with its own page; the card lands on every one. |
| "Highlight the autopilot." | Its own scene and its own narration line. |
| "Play the voices one by one and I'll pick." | Three voices generated; Matilda chosen; every beat retimed to her words. |
| "It says 'and again' on screen instead of 'again'." | The word on screen is always the exact word spoken. |
| "My name at the end, subtle." | A small line at the bottom of the end card. |

The two rules behind all five: **what is said must visibly happen**, and **the text on screen is the
exact word being said.**

## Traps, and what fixed them

| Trap | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Several tabs in one headless window: screenshots of background tabs never return. | One browser context and one window per worker. |
| A CSS transition or a timer anywhere: frames differ between workers. | Everything from `t`. No exceptions. |
| A font weight not loaded yet: the first frames use a fallback font. | Load every weight before the first frame (`film({ fonts })`); the renderer warns about any that aren't. |
| Windows ffmpeg has no glob input for image sequences. | Pipe JPEG frames straight into ffmpeg (`image2pipe`). |
| A loudness filter that adapts as it goes makes music pump. | Measure the whole mix once, apply one fixed gain, catch peaks with a limiter. |
| Fast moves with 4 sub-frames show steps instead of a smear. | More sub-frames for those shots, or a shorter move. |
| A new narration line in the middle shifts everything after it. | Keep later beats relative to one offset constant. |
| Uploads refuse files over 10 MB. | A separate web cut at 30 fps with a higher CRF. |
